Sales Operations & Methodology
Structuring B2B Workflows Before Your First Sales Hire

In early-stage DeepTech ventures, hiring a full-time sales closer often comes after establishing initial market validation. During this interim phase, technical leads and founding teams must conduct their own client interactions directly.

The approach is strictly methodological, educational, and operational:

  • Pedagogy over Pitching: The goal is not to replace salespeople or to close deals on behalf of the team, but to teach technical founders the operational methods of B2B sales enablement.
  • Tooling & Infrastructure: Setting up clean CRM pipelines, lead qualification tools, and structured prospecting frameworks.
  • Preparation & Rehearsal: Conducting meeting simulations, drafting discovery questionnaires, and refining meeting structures.

The Outcome: Technical teams gain the structure, methodology, and confidence to lead their own early B2B meetings effectively until they are ready to onboard a dedicated sales hire.

Tech Transfer & Translation
Parallel Realities: Why DeepTech Translation Requires More Than Shared Vocabulary

Constructing a bridge between scientific research and enterprise market delivery is not a simple communication exercise. Concepts that appear identical across academic and business contexts serve fundamentally different operating systems.

Understanding these false friends is essential to prevent operational misalignment:

  • Peer-Reviewer vs. B2B Client: Your academic supervisor asks you to anticipate every reviewer's objection; your business mentor teaches you to ask the right questions to uncover the client's true operational pain points.
  • Co-Author vs. Business Partner: A co-author strives for scientific originality and conceptual completeness; a business partner seeks risk mitigation, scope adherence, and predictable delivery.
  • Business Coach vs. Academic Educator: A business coach transfers practical tools and methods to solve an immediate operational gap; an academic educator validates the acquisition and mastery of theoretical knowledge (whether acquired before or after the course).
  • Scientific Demonstrator vs. Product MVP: A prototype proves technical feasibility in a controlled setup; an MVP proves that end users can perform tasks reliably and independently.

Playing the role of the "Bridge" means translating these expectations without compromising scientific rigor or enterprise delivery requirements.

Leadership & Delivery Management
Situational Leadership: Calibrating Management Styles Past Apparent Confidence

The Hersey-Blanchard Situational Leadership model provides a structured framework to adapt management styles based on a collaborator's competence and commitment regarding a specific task.

The Observed Fact: The Gap Between Discourse and Action

It is not uncommon for a collaborator to display a high level of assurance and apparent mastery during initial alignments on a new task.

Practical Outcomes: The Limits of the Directing Style

The accuracy of the initial diagnosis is tested during the execution phase. On the ground, a specific pattern may emerge where a purely directive approach reaches its limits: instructions do not lead to deliverables, ownership of the task is bypassed, and project progress stalls.

Operational Alignment: Rectifying the Leadership Trajectory

  • Acknowledge that previous instructions delivered via a directive mode (Style 1) did not generate the targeted operational output.
  • Pivot to Style 2 (Coaching) to re-explain objectives, systematically verify practical understanding, and closely support the technical onboarding.
